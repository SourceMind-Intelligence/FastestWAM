Both A and C used the same foundation checkpoint, LIBERO training data, seed 42, four GPUs per run, five epochs, and final optimizer step 42730. Their action objective and refinement training differed. The logged training wall time was approximately 15 h 49 min for A and 27 h 21 min for C (about 1.73 times as long). Equal optimizer steps therefore do not mean equal training compute.

The saved [A configuration](A_fm/2026-09-25_00-54-12/config.yaml) and [C configuration](C_mip_mixed/2026-09-25_00-54-11/config.yaml), together with their [A](A_fm/hydra/train.log) and [C](C_mip_mixed/hydra/train.log) training logs, preserve this provenance. The three evaluation directories above each contain their own `manifest.json` and `summary.json`.

At inference, the MIP branch uses its fixed two action passes and does not execute FM's iterative joint video/action denoising loop. The `--denoise-steps 10` entry in the C manifest does not turn MIP into a 10-pass action policy. FM-2 versus C is a deployed-policy comparison at two action passes, not an objective-only causal ablation. End-to-end latency was not measured here.

The earlier released OpenWAM FM-2 and Tengfei MIP-2 full-suite scores use different checkpoints and training histories. They are context, not a controlled A/C comparison. This run covers one precision task; it does not establish overall LIBERO performance.
